The position

A payment gateway is not a remittance rail.

The question you asked was whether we can integrate a third party for the transaction. The answer is yes — and it is the right call. But the three names that come up first solve three different problems, and only one of them solves yours.

Collection only

Stripe

Excellent at taking a card payment. It does not convert GBP to INR on your behalf and pay a stranger's Indian bank account — that is a regulated money-movement activity outside its model. It can sit on the pay-in leg, but its published India pricing runs 4.3% for international cards plus 2% for currency conversion, which is fatal to remittance margins.

Payout only

RazorpayX

Genuinely good at the last mile: API-driven payouts to Indian bank accounts and UPI over IMPS, NEFT and RTGS. But it starts from money that is already INR, already inside India, already yours. It does not solve GBP collection, FX, or the cross-border leg — which is the entire regulated middle of your product.

End to end

Wise Platform

Built for exactly this shape: quote, recipient, transfer, funding, tracking and reconciliation across the border, in one contract. Its INR support covers both Indian bank accounts and UPI addresses natively, with Confirmation of Payee built into recipient creation. This is the rail we recommend.

The trap in this decision

Wiring Stripe to your own bank account and then RazorpayX out of it looks like an integration. It is not — it is a home-made cross-border money transmission business with none of the permissions and all of the liability. Money must not touch a corporate account between collection and payout. The whole point of choosing a rail is that the regulated leg belongs to a firm licensed to carry it.

Why Wise came first

  • One contract covers collection, conversion, cross-border movement and INR payout — no stitching, and no gap where the money is nobody's responsibility
  • India is a first-class corridor, with published handling for both bank-account recipients (IFSC and account number) and UPI addresses
  • Confirmation of Payee runs automatically when a recipient is created, which is exactly the control the UK reimbursement regime pushes you towards
  • Transfer requirements are returned by the API per corridor, so the app renders the right fields without hard-coding rules that change
  • Partner pricing configuration means your fee is your own, not a rebate on someone else's

Why we still name a second

Provider concentration is the largest single operational risk in this architecture. If the rail declines your model, changes pricing, or has a bad week, a single-provider platform has no answer.

We therefore recommend Nium as the designated second rail, and design the integration so that adding it is a contained piece of work rather than a re-architecture. We are not proposing to build it now — we are proposing not to make it impossible later.

Thunes remains the natural third if cash pickup or wallet payout ever enters scope.

Wise Platform

Which door we walk through matters more than the API.

Wise Platform is not one product. It offers distinct integration models, and they differ in who holds the licence, who owns the customer, and who performs identity verification. This is the single most important thing to resolve before development starts.

The condition we will not paper over

Wise's own documentation is explicit that third-party partners — those moving money on behalf of their customers — need either a sponsor bank or their own licence under which funds can be moved to Wise. Choosing Wise removes an enormous amount of engineering. It does not remove the need for a regulated position in the UK.

This is why we are not promising a four-month build. The engineering can move fast. The commercial and regulatory qualification with Wise runs on Wise's calendar, and it is the gate that decides your launch date. We start it in week one and run it in parallel with everything else.

What Wise carries

  • Live GBP→INR quoting, with rate, fee and delivery estimate returned together
  • Recipient creation, format validation and name-match confirmation

  • Your support experience when a transfer sticks

The honest question

If Wise does all that, what are we paying you for?

A fair question, and one your board will ask. The answer is that Wise sells a rail. It does not sell a remittance business. Everything a customer actually experiences, and everything a regulator actually inspects, sits on our side of the line.

Our side of the line

Identity & sessionYour customer, your login, your device trust, your step-up authentication meeting UK strong customer authentication rules.
Presentation & disclosureRate, fee, total cost and delivery time shown before commitment, in the form the Consumer Duty expects. Wise returns numbers; how they are framed is a compliance obligation you own.
Internal ledgerDouble-entry, immutable, in your books. Wise's records are a counterparty statement, not your accounts. Every rupee delivered must trace to a pound collected, in a system you control.
Fraud & reimbursement postureWarning journeys, staged limits for new customers, behavioural scoring and a claims process. The reimbursement liability lands on the sending firm, not on the rail.
Operations consoleStuck transfers, refunds, disputes, case management, four-eyes controls, audit trail. This is where your team actually works.
Reconciliation & reportingDaily match of your ledger against the provider statement, exception ageing, management information and regulatory return preparation.

Their side of the line

Identity verificationDocument and identity checks executed under Wise's programme against the profiles we create.
Pricing engineLive mid-market rate, transfer fee and delivery estimate returned as a single quote.
Recipient validationFormat checks on IFSC codes and UPI addresses, account existence and name matching, returned with a confidence outcome.
Regulated movementCollection, conversion, cross-border settlement and INR delivery — the legs that require a licence.
StatusTransfer state changes delivered by webhook for us to project into your ledger and your app.

The proportion is roughly two to one. Integrating the rail is about a tenth of the programme. Building a business that can operate, reconcile, defend and report on what the rail carries is the rest. That ratio is why the saving from choosing Wise is real but moderate — and why anyone quoting you a four-month remittance platform is quoting the integration and quietly omitting the business.

Design principle

Built to survive a change of provider.

We recommend Wise and we expect it to serve you well. We also refuse to build an application that cannot survive changing its mind.

The adapter layer

Our services never call Wise directly. They call a canonical interface — quote, createRecipient, verifyRecipient, createTransfer, fund, status, refund — expressed in your domain language, not the provider's. A single Wise adapter does the translating.

Provider identifiers, state names and error codes are mapped into a canonical model at the boundary. Nothing provider-specific reaches the ledger, the app or the console. That mapping table is a deliverable, reviewed and versioned.

What it buys you

  • A second rail becomes an adapter plus certification — weeks of work, not a rebuild
  • Corridor-level routing later: by provider, by amount band, by cost, by availability
  • A real negotiating position at contract renewal, because switching is credible
  • Continuity if a provider has an outage or declines a transaction class
  • Provider changes never force a mobile app release

Cost of the principle: roughly ₹4 lakh in the recommended scope, spent on the interface, the mapping layer and a contract-test suite that runs against a simulated provider. We consider it the highest-return four lakh in the programme. It is also what makes the simulated provider possible — which means engineering never idles while Wise onboarding is in progress.

What did not get cheaper

The ledger, the operations console, reconciliation, the mobile applications, the fraud posture, security assurance and QA are effectively unchanged. They are functions of your business and your obligations, not of which rail carries the money.

We want to be plain about this rather than let an integration decision be mistaken for a discount that isn't there. A remittance platform that skips these does not become cheaper — it becomes unlaunchable, and the cost reappears later at a worse moment.

Next step

One question to Wise, before anything else.

Everything in this document depends on an answer only Wise can give. We propose getting it first, in week one, with a pack we prepare on your behalf.

The qualification pack

A short, precise submission that lets Wise route you to the right integration model instead of a generic sales conversation. It states:

  • Corridor and direction — UK to India, GBP to INR
  • Originator and beneficiary — consumer to consumer, personal remittance
  • Payout types required — Indian bank account and UPI address
  • Expected monthly volume, and average, median and maximum ticket size
  • The client's current and intended regulated position in the UK
  • Whether a sponsor bank arrangement exists or is being sought
  • The single question: which integration model applies to us, and on what terms?

What we do while we wait

Nothing stops. The qualification runs in parallel with a discovery phase that produces the artefacts the build needs regardless of which rail is finally selected.

  • Canonical provider interface and mapping specification
  • A simulated rail, so engineering can start against a working contract
  • Transfer state machine and ledger design
  • Nium approached in parallel as a genuine alternative
  • Tested UX prototype, including the name-match journey
  • Fixed-price statement of work for the full build
